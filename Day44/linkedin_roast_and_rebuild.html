7-Day LinkedIn Activation Plan

Day 2Publish Post #1 — "I rebuilt my LinkedIn"
⌄

Hook drives comments by asking a direct question at the end.

I just rewrote my entire LinkedIn profile — and it made me realize how badly I was burying my own story. For 2 years I've been deep in the GenAI stack: RAG, agentic systems, LangChain, LangGraph. That part was easy to write. The part I almost left out? Before any of this, I spent 2 years doing COVID-19 research at ICMR and was recognized as a Corona Warrior for it. Turns out that's not a footnote — it's the reason I build AI for healthcare specifically, and it's the thing that actually makes my profile different from every other "GenAI Engineer" headline out there. If you're also sitting on a background that doesn't "obviously" connect to your current work — it might be your biggest asset, not something to hide. What's the unconventional part of YOUR background that you've been underselling on LinkedIn?
Day 3Send 10 strategic connection requests
⌄

Who to target: GenAI/AI Engineers at healthcare-AI startups, ML recruiters who post about AI hiring, engineering managers at companies using LangChain/RAG in production (check their job posts), and people who post about agentic AI in your feed.

Hi [Name] — saw your work on [specific thing they posted/built]. I'm building agentic AI & RAG systems with a healthcare focus (ex-ICMR researcher). Would love to connect and follow your work.
Day 4Comment on 5 posts in your target space
⌄

The Value Comment formula: Agree + Add Insight + Ask a Question.

Example: "Agree that RAG-only pipelines break down on multi-hop questions — I ran into this building a healthcare-domain RAG project and found reranking helped more than adding retrieval steps. Curious if you've tried reranking here, or gone a different route?"

Day 5Publish Post #2 — lessons-learned / hot take
⌄
Unpopular opinion after building 30+ GenAI projects: most "agentic AI" demos I see are just a chatbot with extra steps. A real agent needs to handle failure — retries, tool errors, bad retrievals — not just the happy path. The projects of mine that actually taught me something were the ones where the first version broke in production-like testing, and I had to redesign the control flow, not just the prompt. If you're learning agentic AI right now: build for the failure case first. The happy path is the easy 20%. What's the most "it broke and taught me something" moment you've had building with LLMs?
Day 6Engage + send 5 more connection requests
⌄

Reply to every comment on both posts within a few hours — engagement in the first 24 hours affects reach. Send 5 more targeted requests using the Day 3 template, this time to people who engaged with your posts.

Day 7Review profile views & connection acceptance rate
⌄

What the numbers mean: A jump in profile views after Day 1 = your headline/keywords are working in search. A low connection acceptance rate (below ~40%) usually means your note is too generic or you're targeting too broadly — tighten the "why you" in the template. Post impressions with low comments = the hook isn't sparking a reaction; the ending question matters more than people think.
