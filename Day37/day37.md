# Day 37 — Task Compass: Learn How Work Flows Through Real Organizations

## 🎯 What I Built

**Task Compass** is a management simulation game that teaches organizational thinking — ownership, delegation, workflow, and collaboration through interactive gameplay instead of theory or quizzes.

For this challenge, I generated the **Hospital / Healthcare** version of Task Compass, where every task card, role card, and scenario is grounded in real healthcare workplace situations (Doctors, Nurses, Admin, Pharmacists, Radiology, Lab, IT, HR, Quality, Social Work, Billing, Security).

## 🕹️ How It Works

The simulation runs across 3 progressively realistic stages:

1. **Stage 1 – Who Owns This?**
   A workplace task is presented (e.g., a patient safety issue). I had to drag the correct role into the ownership slot and see *why* that role owns it and who assists.

2. **Stage 2 – Task Routing**
   A task moves through the hospital. I had to build the correct workflow order (e.g., Nurse → Doctor → Pharmacist → Patient) and watch it animate through the organization.

3. **Stage 3 – Collaboration Challenge**
   Bigger, messier problems (e.g., a sudden drop in patient satisfaction) required assigning up to 4 departments instead of a single owner — reinforcing that complex problems are rarely solved by one person.

At the end, an **Organizational Thinking Dashboard** displays a radar chart and skill bars across 4 categories: Ownership, Delegation, Collaboration, and Workflow followed by a personal reflection.

## 📚 Key Learnings

- **Ownership ≠ working alone.** Even in collaborative environments like hospitals, someone still needs to be clearly accountable, ambiguity around ownership is what actually causes delays, not collaboration itself.
- **Workflow order matters.** The sequence work moves through an organization (support → diagnosis → specialist → resolution) isn't arbitrary, it exists to catch errors early and route decisions to the right expertise level.
- **Collaboration scales with complexity.** Simple tasks have one clear owner; the moment a problem becomes systemic (e.g., dropping patient satisfaction), the right move is to spread responsibility across departments (Nursing, Admin, Quality, IT) rather than pin it on one team.
- **Simulations teach better than quizzes.** Framing this as "own it / route it / collaborate on it" rather than "pick the right answer" made the organizational logic stick in a way a multiple-choice quiz never would.
